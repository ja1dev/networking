Terminal labs

Real CLI practice in your browser. Each lab is a small network of IOS-style routers and switches and VPCS PCs, with a live topology diagram. You configure it at the console exactly as you would in EVE-NG, and every task is checked against what the network actually does: a ping only succeeds if your VLANs, trunks, routes and ACLs really let it through.

What the simulator covers

Simulated: user/privileged/config modes with ?, Tab, abbreviations, no and do; interfaces, subinterfaces and loopbacks; VLANs, access ports, 802.1Q trunks with native and allowed VLANs, and DTP negotiation; router-on-a-stick; switch SVIs and default gateways; static, default and floating static routes; single-process OSPFv2 with areas, DR/BDR election, timers, passive interfaces, costs and default-information originate; standard and extended ACLs with match counters; DHCP server, exclusions and ip helper-address relay; console and VTY passwords, local users, SSH and telnet between devices; CDP, MAC and ARP tables; ping and traceroute; saving, reload and | include/section.

Not simulated: STP, EtherChannel, IPv6, NAT, HSRP, EIGRP/BGP, port security and wireless. Use EVE-NG or the workbook labs for those. Commands outside the list answer with the usual % Invalid input, just as an IOS image without the feature would.
